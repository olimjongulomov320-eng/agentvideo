## A. The concept

- **Title:** *Ko'prik* (The Bridge) — working title. Alt: *Paper and Code*.
- **Central message:** For sixteen years this firm has closed the distance between people and the law — first in the courtroom, now in the code. The new office is not more space; it is a bigger stage for that work.
- **Narrative in one line:** A small 2010 Tashkent practice that chose the hard specialisations, earned international trust, and then set out to build the tools that will deliver law in Uzbekistan's next decade.

## D. New-office footage — purpose and rules

The new office (Mirobod ko'chasi 46a) is **not a tour**. It is the physical proof of a change of stage.
- Shoot it as a **threshold being crossed** (Scene 7): the camera enters as a person would.
- First **empty** (potential), then **filled** (people) — the same room in two states.
- Include **details that mean something**: the new sign, keys, a first file placed on a first desk, morning light.
- **Never imply a historical event** with newly staged footage. Founding-era scenes use archive assets only; if archive is thin, use texture and light — not a staged "old office" with current staff.
- Close on a **team wide shot** in the new space (Scene 8) — the firm as it is now.

---

## E. Ending

Final spoken line: **"…Ish davom etadi."** / **"…Работа продолжается."**
It connects the past (2010 experience), the present (2026 technology) and the future (Uzbekistan's law) in three words, and it is honest — it promises continuity, not a superlative. The logo lands as the last chord rings, then cut to black.
