## Concept A — "The Advocate Who Built an AI"

**Central idea:** A lawyer who spent years defending people in Uzbek courtrooms is now helping to build the legal system's next layer — an AI that answers legal questions in three languages from the country's own law database. The new office is not a bigger room; it is a platform for a different kind of practice.

**Opening hook (first 8–10s):** A courtroom corridor — or an empty chair and a stack of case files — cut hard to a blinking terminal: a legal answer generated in Uzbek, cited to `lex.uz`. The question hangs: *who builds the technology that answers the law?* A lawyer.

**Narrative structure:** Thesis → proof → human cost → present.
1. The advocate (who he is, what he has seen).
2. The turn: he stopped accepting that legal knowledge should be slow to reach people.
3. The build: Wakil AI — Stanford CodeX, Academy of Justice.
4. The people behind the practice.
5. The new office as the moment the practice and the technology share an address.

**Emotional progression:** Respect → surprise → conviction → pride/forward momentum.

**Strongest verified evidence:** Wakil AI in Stanford CodeX TechIndex `[PB-07]`; Academy of Justice presentation `[PB-11]`; founder's 2025 talk on AI in legal practice `[PB-13]`; firm identity `[PB-01, PB-03]`.

**Interview use:** Abdulloh Sodiq carries the spine (guide `06`, Q10–12). Prof. Gulyamov adds the second voice on cyber law (guide `07` A).

**Visual approach:** Two visual worlds edited against each other — paper (files, stamps, corridors) and screen (code, lex.uz, queries). Restrained, no futuristic clichés. Real software, real hands.

**History ↔ new office:** The new office becomes the physical answer to the film's question — the place where old practice and new technology finally sit in one building.

**Strengths:** Externally verified (rare); unmistakably about *this* firm; contemporary; works even with limited archive.
**Weaknesses / gaps:** Depends on the founder's willingness to speak and on consent to show Wakil AI. Needs the new-office address and B-roll. If the firm wants a *history* film, this leans future — must be balanced with a short origin beat.

## Concept B — "Two Doors, One Practice"

**Central idea:** A move is a moment when a firm looks back. Through the old door (Yakkasaroy 5) walked 15 years of clients; through the new door walks a specialist firm. The film follows what is *carried* across — not furniture, but habits, promises, people.

**Opening hook:** A last box lifted from the old office; the old sign coming down. Voice: *"What do you take with you when you leave the place you built?"*

**Narrative structure:** Chronological, deliberately simple.
1. 2010 — one advocate begins.
2. The work that built trust (specific, anonymised).
3. 2019 — the partnership takes its present name.
4. 2026 — the move; the team; the new room.
5. The promise that does not move.

**Emotional progression:** Nostalgia → respect → resolve → welcome.

**Strongest verified evidence:** firm identity and specialisation `[PB-01, PB-03]`; the 2019 name milestone (user-confirmed); historic address `[PB-09]`.
**Interview use:** Heavy — needs both partners and, ideally, two team members for texture.

**Visual approach:** Archive-led. Old office, old documents, old photographs layered with the new space. Cut on diptychs (old room / new room, same light).
**History ↔ new office:** The office *is* the narrative device — the move is the whole story.

**Strengths:** Warm, classic, easy to follow; suits the event.
**Weaknesses / gaps:** **Currently under-evidenced** — it needs archive photos and dated early documents we do not yet have. Without them it becomes generic "moving day" footage. Weakest of the three on distinctive content.

## Concept C — "The Client at the Door"

**Central idea:** Strip away the portraits and the plaques: a law firm exists because at some point a person — frightened, or a business owner, or a family — needed someone to stand between them and the machinery. The film is about the human meaning of that work, and about a firm that has done it for over a decade.

**Opening hook:** A single sentence from a real (anonymised, consented) client situation, over an empty consultation chair. *"They told me I had no case."*

**Narrative structure:**
1. A human problem.
2. The advocate enters.
3. What competence actually looks like (one anonymised matter).
4. A decade of the same promise.
5. The new office as an open door for the next person.

**Emotional progression:** Tension → relief → trust → invitation.

**Strongest verified evidence:** the firm's stated mission and practice areas `[PB-01]` — but **no cleared case material exists yet.**
**Interview use:** Depends on the firm releasing at least one anonymised matter, approved for public use.

**Visual approach:** Intimate, close, faces and hands, consultations, documents — little architecture.
**History ↔ new office:** The new office is framed as the door more people can walk through.

**Strengths:** Highest emotional ceiling — this is what a law firm *is for*.
**Weaknesses / gaps:** **Highest risk.** It requires case material that is publishable, which is exactly what confidentiality rules make hard. Without a cleared story it collapses into generic sentiment. **I do not recommend it until a cleared matter is confirmed.**

---

## Recommendation — **Concept A**

**Why, given the evidence actually collected:**
1. It is the only concept whose strongest asset is **verified by independent third parties** (Stanford CodeX, a government academy) — so it survives the managing partner's scrutiny.
2. It is **specific to this firm**; Concepts B and C could be made about almost any practice.
3. It works with what we *have* (public record + interviews) rather than waiting on archive material we do not yet have.
4. It gives the new office a real meaning: a practice confident enough to shape how law is delivered next — not just a nicer address.

**Best balance:** A (dominant) with a short founding beat borrowed from B (the 2010 founding; activity 2011, recognition 2012), so the film still honours the firm's history. That hybrid is what I would shoot.

## Guard rails
No concept may rest on an unverified claim, a fabricated founding story, or a superlative the evidence does not support. All three above are written to be filmable with consent and without invention.
